I am an audio AI and neural engineering researcher, and a Ph.D. student in Electrical Engineering at Columbia University, where I work with Prof. Nima Mesgarani in the Neural Acoustic Processing Lab as a Blavatnik Presidential Fellow.

I build real-time wearable speech enhancement systems — models that let a listener pick out the voices they care about in a crowded room, small and fast enough to run on the device in their ear. My longer-term goal is to make that selection controllable through brain signals, so the system knows who you are trying to listen to rather than having to be told.

Alongside that, I work on interpretability of audio models — what speech representations inside a neural network have in common with those in the human auditory cortex — and on multimodal LLMs, including their post-training and evaluation.

Before Columbia, I graduated from Duke University in 2024 with a B.S.E. in Biomedical Engineering, a B.S.E. in Electrical and Computer Engineering, and a B.S. in Mathematics, with minors in Computer Science and Philosophy. I worked on brain–computer interfaces and biomedical signal processing with Prof. Pratik Chhatbar, Prof. Leslie Collins, and Prof. Hau-Tieng Wu.

Outside of research, I lift, run, play tennis, and teach weekly math and science lessons to elementary school students in Harlem. One of my favorite things about New York is that so many of its museums are free.
